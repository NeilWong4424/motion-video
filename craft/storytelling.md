# Storytelling

## Purpose / Use when

Use when planning the narrative arc of a film — turning a Brief's facts and single message into a
scene-by-scene story that a viewer *feels*, not merely reads. This module is the director's-brain counterpart
to the mechanical `narrativeArc` enum: it teaches how to build an arc that moves someone, so the Treatment's
beats carry emotional intent, not just information states.

## Reads

Read the approved Brief (goal, single message, audience, facts) and, when present, an intended emotional
takeaway assumption. Read `emotional-pacing.md` and `continuity-first.md`. Use the role-owned context supplied
by the loader.

## Writes

None. This module performs no artifact writes. It informs the Treatment the creative-direction role owns.

## Must

- **State one promise, then deliver it.** Make a single emotional promise in the opening beat ("something
  monumental is coming") and pay it off in the final beat ("…and it comes down to one"). Every beat between is
  the journey from that promise to that payoff. If a beat does not advance the promise, cut it.
- **Give every beat one thing to say to the audience.** For each beat, name the felt outcome (what the viewer
  should *feel*) and the unspoken line (what it says). "Astonishment at scale — *this many, never before.*"
- **Choose the smallest arc that still MOVES someone.** Minimalism serves clarity, not timidity. Prefer the
  fewest beats that still build → turn → resolve. This deliberately overrides any instruction to pick the
  "smallest honest arc" as mere information states: honest AND affecting.
- **Show the feeling before the fact.** Let the audience feel a truth (mass filling the frame) an instant
  before it is labeled ("48"). Emotion first, data second.
- **Carry one through-line.** Prefer a single hero / through-object that persists across beats and bridges
  (true shared identity), giving the film one protagonist and satisfying the seamless-motion gate as story,
  not compliance.
- **Map the arc to typed narrative functions.** Express the story as ordered steps
  (`hook → setup → development → turn → payoff → resolve → cta` as applicable), each with an objective, so the
  structured `narrativeArc` is actually populated rather than reduced to one flat sentence.

## Must not

- Reduce the arc to a slideshow of facts, a bullet list of features, or a sequence of unrelated beats.
- Invent facts, claims, dates, people, rights, or CTAs to manufacture drama; drama comes from ordering and
  emphasis of *verified* material, never fabrication.
- Add beats that do not advance the single promise, or a payoff the setup never earned.
- Treat "smallest" as "flattest" — never trade all emotional shape for brevity.

## Stop conditions

Stop when a proposed beat contradicts the Brief's single message, requires an unverified fact, or cannot name
its audience takeaway. Route missing capability through the capability-gap workflow, not a weaker story.

## Output schema

None.

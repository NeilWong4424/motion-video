# Emotional Pacing

## Purpose / Use when

Use when deciding beat durations, hold lengths, stagger, and the rhythm of reveals — pacing as *feeling*, not
only legibility. This module tunes the tempo of the arc so the film breathes: builds tension, lands payoffs,
and never feels either rushed or dead.

## Reads

Read the approved Brief duration, the Treatment's narrative arc and beat intentions, the selected motion
profile, `storytelling.md`, and `motion-craft.md`. Use the role-owned context supplied by the loader.

## Writes

None. This module performs no artifact writes.

## Must

- **Put beats on the down-beat, bridges on the breath.** Holds are the landings (long enough to read and
  *land* the feeling); bridges are the up-beats between (quick enough to feel alive). The cut should be felt,
  not seen.
- **Match duration to emotional job.** A hook is punchy; an awe beat hits hard and holds; a change beat is
  snappy; the beat that resolves order is the longest and most choreographed; a climax builds then releases.
- **Choreograph, don't dump.** Reveal grouped elements on a stagger (roughly 30–60 ms between units) so a set
  feels conducted, not simultaneous. Simultaneous mass reveals read cheap.
- **Use build/release curves.** Ease into tension (accelerate), ease out of payoff (decelerate and settle).
  Prefer eased entrances with follow-through over linear or hold-snapped motion everywhere.
- **Give the payoff air.** Reserve the longest, quietest hold for the single climax so it registers; do not
  step on it with a competing move.
- **Keep total rhythm coherent.** The sequence of hold/transition lengths should form a felt cadence across the
  whole film, not a random set of durations.

## Must not

- Fill time with constant movement, decorative drift, or motion for its own sake.
- Reveal a whole group at once when a staggered choreography would read as considered.
- Snap hero motion with `hold`/`linear` when eased entrances with settle are available.
- Impose universal timing numbers detached from the selected motion profile; ranges are starting points.

## Stop conditions

Stop when a proposed tempo contradicts readability, the motion profile, the message, or the duration budget.
A beat that cannot be read at its hold length is too short regardless of desired energy.

## Output schema

None.
